Part 2

Drag the linkers into the text

Twelve gaps, fourteen linkers. Two are not needed. Grammar decides most of them: one gap takes a noun phrase, two are followed by inversion, one takes an -ing form. The colours stay hidden until you check, then each gap tells you what job it was doing.

Nobody goes out any more

There was a time when a Friday needed no planning. You turned up, and so did everyone else. 1Contrast, a night out now takes three weeks of negotiation in a group chat and still falls apart the day before. 2Concession the apps designed to bring us together, getting six people into the same room has never been harder.

The usual explanation is money, and it is not wrong. A round now costs what a meal used to, 3Result most of us do the sums before we answer. 4Concession, the maths is not the whole story. Plenty of people cancel on nights they have already paid for.

The honest answer is that staying in got better. Streaming, delivery and a decent sofa have raised the standard of doing nothing, 5Result raising the bar for anything that asks you to put shoes on. 6Concession the pull of the sofa may be, nobody has ever come home from a night in with a story.

The cost lands unevenly. People with partners and small children lose an evening. People living alone lose most of their week, 7Cause the invitations quietly stop once you have turned down three in a row. 8Result is the pull of the default that a whole evening can disappear without anyone deciding anything.

9Concession the problem is partly a matter of prices and timetables, it is also just a habit, and habits can be interrupted. 10Result, the fix is not a better app but a lower bar: one person naming a day and a place, 11Contrast waiting for the group to agree. Say yes before you have time to talk yourself out of it, 12Condition the evening quietly becomes another one at home.

What follows the linker

PatternLinkersExample
+ clausealthough, even though, whereas, whilst, much as, inasmuch as, insofar as, on the grounds that, seeing that, provided that, lest, for fear that, so much so that, with the result that, to the point whereMuch as I admire her work, I cannot defend this.
+ noun or -ingdespite, in spite of, notwithstanding, for all, owing to, on account of, in view of, by virtue of, given, coupled with, not to mention, with a view to, as againstFor all the scale of the change, there was little resistance.
Sentence adverbhowever, nevertheless, nonetheless, conversely, by contrast, even so, be that as it may, that said, accordingly, hence, thus, thereby, to that end, by the same token, added to this, in essence, if anythingThe data were thin. Be that as it may, the policy went ahead.
Inversion followsno sooner ... than, hardly ... when, scarcely ... when, not only ... but also, nor, little did, so + adjective ... that, such was ... that, only then, were it not for, should you, had I knownSo strong were the objections that the scheme was dropped.
Fronted concessionadjective + though / as, whatever, however much, be it ... orCompelling though the case may be, it changes who the land is for.

despite of and notwithstanding of do not exist. The words take a noun straight after them.

although the traffic needs a subject and a verb. Use despite the traffic or although the traffic was heavy.

It was late, however we arrived on time. "However" is not a conjunction. Use a full stop and a comma, or swap in but.

On the contrary contradicts a statement. It does not compare two people or two places. That job belongs to whereas or by contrast.

albeit that she was tired is wrong. "Albeit" takes a phrase: albeit reluctantly, albeit a small one.

No sooner we had sat down when needs inversion and "than": No sooner had we sat down than.

Register. "Insofar as" and "hitherto" in conversation sound like a contract. "Plus" and "mind you" in an essay sound careless. Part 7 sorts them out.
