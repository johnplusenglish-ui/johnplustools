Working the list

How to approach them

Lead with the format, not the band

An agency buying for a product launch is not looking for a wedding singer. They are looking for a named module they can drop into a running order and price. Shanti and Son(g)s already has those: vocal sextet, string quartet, laser harp, sax over electronic pads, brass, DJ handover. Send the format menu first and the showreel second.

Lead with the corporate wall

American Express, Johnson and Johnson, Huawei, Lottomatica, BCC and Isokinetic are already on the client wall. That removes the risk question before it is asked. Name two of them in the first two lines of any email to an agency.

Write to a person

Twenty-eight of these entries carry a named contact, usually a sales, business development or events director. A message to info@ at a 180-person agency goes nowhere. Use the named address where there is one, and copy the general inbox.

Ask to be a supplier, not a booking

The ask is to be added to the supplier list for live entertainment, not to be booked for a specific date. Agencies keep rosters and pull from them. Offer a rate card by format, a tech rider and a one-page PDF they can forward internally.

The season matters

Italian corporate calendars cluster in late spring (May and June conventions) and in November and December (kick-offs, awards nights, Christmas parties). Approach in January and in September, which is when agencies are building budgets rather than running events.

Respect the cold-contact rules

These are business contacts published by the companies themselves for commercial contact, which is a normal basis for B2B outreach in Italy. Identify who you are in the first line, say where you got the address, and give a working unsubscribe or opt-out line. Do not buy, scrape or guess personal addresses beyond what the companies publish.

Where this came from

Method and caveats

Read this before you send anything

  • Contacts were taken from public sources. Company websites, their own contact pages, trade association member listings and public business directories. Nothing was taken from behind a login or a paywall.
  • Phone numbers drift. Several agencies publish two or three numbers across different directories, usually because they moved or centralised a switchboard. Where that happened the entry notes it. Check the website before dialling.
  • Named people move. Roles were accurate at the time of the source page, and some of those pages are two or three years old. Verify a name on LinkedIn before writing to it.
  • The fit rating is a judgement, not data. It is based on what each agency says it does. A medium rating is not a reason to skip an agency, only a reason to put it second.
  • This list is not exhaustive. The main Italian MICE directory alone lists 86 suppliers in Rome and several hundred nationally. This is the filtered top of that pool plus the national agencies that matter.

What is deliberately not here

  • Wedding planners. The brief was corporate agencies, and the luxury destination wedding planner list is a separate job worth doing on its own.
  • Venues. Villa Miani, Villa Aurelia and the rest already know the band, and venue in-house teams buy differently from agencies.
  • Hostess, catering, stand builders and AV suppliers, which fill out the directories but do not commission music.
  • Agencies with no public contact point, which were dropped rather than guessed at.

Suggested order of work

  • Start with the sixteen Rome agencies rated high fit that carry a named contact. That is a fortnight of work and the warmest ground.
  • Then the Rome DMCs, which sell Italy to foreign corporate clients and need local suppliers who can deal in English.
  • Then Milan, where the budgets are larger but the competition is heavier and a Rome address is a weaker card.
  • Keep Kampaay for last and go through its supplier channel rather than an email, because it works as a platform.
